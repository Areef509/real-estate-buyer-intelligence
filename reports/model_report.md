_Generated by `run_pipeline.py`; every number below is computed from the data._

## 1. Dataset

- `clients`: 2,000 rows x 12 cols, 0 missing cells, 0 duplicate rows
- `properties`: 10,000 rows x 10 cols, 2,695 missing cells, 0 duplicate rows
- `buyers`: 2,000 rows x 29 cols, 0 missing cells, 0 duplicate rows
- The 2,695 missing `client_ref` values are exactly the *Available* (unsold) listings, not data errors; 0 clients have no purchase and every client has >= 3 purchases.
- Transactions span 2024-01-01 to 2025-12-01.

## 2. Key data finding (read this first)

Behavioural, financing, intent and satisfaction variables are nearly statistically independent (most pairwise |r| < 0.1). On the selected model the silhouette is **0.173**, versus **0.158** on column-shuffled data with the same marginals (gap = +0.015). The data therefore contain **little natural cluster structure**: segments here are *useful partitions of a continuum*, not sharply separated populations. Including tower diversification would isolate a 65-buyer micro-segment (the only buyers with units in more than one tower pattern) rather than describe general buyer behaviour, so that feature is excluded from the clustering and reported in profiles instead.

## 5. Final model

- Algorithm: **K-Means**, K = **5**, feature set `behaviour` (selected automatically by the documented rule in `src/selection.py` (baseline weights))
- Silhouette 0.173 | Davies-Bouldin 1.434 | Calinski-Harabasz 389.5
- Stability: bootstrap ARI 0.818 (sd 0.135); random-restart ARI 0.723 (single-init restarts)
- PCA check: reaching 90% variance needs 5 components (all features are nearly uncorrelated), so PCA gives no compression; silhouette 0.173 vs 0.173 in the original space, partition agreement ARI 1.00. PCA is therefore used for visualisation only.
- Gender-inclusion test: adding gender changes the partition little (ARI 0.98); gender is excluded from clustering and kept for the fairness audit.


### How the solution was selected (and how firmly)

- Rule (`src/selection.py`, config `selection:`): only deployable algorithms are eligible; K in 3..6; every segment >= 5% of buyers; score = 0.4 subsample-stability ARI + 0.2 seed ARI + 0.2 silhouette-gap + 0.1 Davies-Bouldin + 0.1 Calinski-Harabasz (last three min-max normalised).
- Selected: **kmeans K=5 [behaviour]**. Best over ALL algorithms (benchmark only): **gmm K=3 [behaviour]** - it is not used because the deployed scorer is nearest-centroid K-Means, so the evaluated model must be the shipped model.
- **Robustness:** the selected solution wins 42% of 24 alternative weight/constraint scenarios. Winners across scenarios: kmeans K=4 [behaviour] (11); kmeans K=5 [behaviour] (10); kmeans K=3 [behaviour] (2); kmeans K=3 [behaviour+customer+geo/channel] (1). See `selection_sensitivity.csv`.
- The choice between K=4 and K=5 depends mainly on the minimum-segment-size constraint, not on the quality metrics. Metrics are within each other's resampling noise (see standard deviations in `model_metrics.csv`).

### Other algorithms

Hierarchical clustering and GMM are evaluated as benchmarks (full table: `model_metrics.csv`). Average linkage: silhouette 0.132-0.319 for K=3..6 (best K=3 with smallest cluster 0.1% of buyers). Complete linkage: silhouette 0.074-0.142 for K=3..6 (best K=3 with smallest cluster 2.4% of buyers). Ward linkage: silhouette 0.118-0.141 for K=3..6 (best K=3 with smallest cluster 5.7% of buyers). GMM: silhouette 0.128-0.184 for K=3..6 (best K=3 with smallest cluster 5.7% of buyers). K-Means: silhouette 0.167-0.177. A very small smallest-cluster share means a high silhouette is obtained by isolating a few buyers, not by finding a usable segmentation. Seed ARI is 1.0 for deterministic algorithms by construction.

The buyer table is rebuilt from `clients_cleaned.csv` and the sold rows of `properties_cleaned.csv`. 27 of 28 shared columns match the provided `buyer_features_model_ready.csv` exactly. Mismatches:

`office_share` in the provided file is not the share of a buyer's purchases that are offices (it is not reproducible from units, area or value), so the pipeline uses the rebuilt value. Currency (USD) and area (sq ft) units are assumed from column names; the source files carry no unit metadata.

## 8. Limitations

- Weak cluster separation (see section 2); segment boundaries are soft and a client near a boundary could sit in the neighbouring segment.
- Financing, investment purpose and satisfaction do not differ materially across behavioural segments, so they are handled at the *individual* level in the app.
- No income, wealth, budget or transaction-channel data exist; nothing in the system infers them.
- Every client has >= 3 purchases and purchases are monthly snapshots (2024-01 to 2025-12); scoring customers with no purchase history is outside the training range (the app warns).
- Cross-sectional data: segments describe the observed period and say nothing about causality or future behaviour.
- K=4 and K=5 cannot be separated by the metrics; the final K is partly a business judgement (minimum segment size).
- Currency and area units are assumed (USD, sq ft); the source tables have no unit metadata.
- Segment-level differences in satisfaction (e.g. the high-volume segment) are exploratory: they do not survive multiple-comparison correction here.
- No business outcome (conversion, retention, satisfaction change) has been measured; recommendations are untested hypotheses until a pilot is run.
